## 5. Search camera source correction (candidate only)

The audited V32 search camera encoder remained near 35 degrees down (about 34.6–35.25 degrees). Camera vertical FOV is about 36.1 degrees. At roughly 6 m altitude and a 0.45 m deck, the expected target depression is about 34 degrees at 8 m horizontal range, 11 degrees at 28 m, and 6 degrees at 52 m. Thus one fixed 35-degree-down scan can see the near boat but place mid/far targets outside the image. This is consistent with actual RGB (mid/far had zero target pixels); it is not permission to infer target presence in the controller.

After approval, implement a new versioned protocol/adapter/runner copy with a bounded elevation schedule of approximately **6, 18, and 35 degrees down**. This is a development hypothesis to verify against camera calibration, not a result. Preserve existing distinct-yaw-bin coverage within each elevation band. Advance to the next band only after a complete 360-degree yaw survey with no valid target; do not reset yaw progress or remain indefinitely on one band. Never advance/claim complete from repeated frames. A valid unresolved candidate follows its existing inspection/recovery path; do not label it wrong merely because recognition is uncertain. Cap at three bands and leave mission/time/return reserve unchanged.

Before any flight, inspect the actual protocol call graph and test that `_new_survey` advances the band only after the required full scan, the next view uses the intended encoder setpoint, each band gets distinct yaw coverage, and no code path can issue descent without current bounded geometry. Check near/mid/far ray/FOV projections offline as diagnosis only; final visibility gate uses exact actual onboard RGB and the separated truth annotator.
